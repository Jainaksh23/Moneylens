# Asset Allocation and Portfolio Diversification

**Author**: MoneyLens team  
**Last Updated**: 2026-10-15  
**Category**: Portfolio Construction  

## What is Asset Allocation?
Asset allocation is the investment strategy of dividing your total financial portfolio among different primary asset categories—predominantly **Equities**, **Fixed Income (Debt)**, **Gold**, and **Cash Equivalents**. Academic research (notably by Brinson, Hood, and Beebower) shows that asset allocation accounts for more than 90% of the variance in portfolio returns over time, far exceeding the impact of individual security selection or market timing.

## Core Asset Classes and Their Roles
1. **Equities (Stocks / Index Funds)**:
   - Primary role: Long-term capital growth and beating inflation.
   - High short-term volatility, high historical long-term real returns.
2. **Debt / Fixed Income (PPF, EPF, Debt Funds, FDs)**:
   - Primary role: Capital preservation, stability, and liquidity.
   - Dampens portfolio drawdowns during stock market crashes.
3. **Gold (Sovereign Gold Bonds, Gold ETFs)**:
   - Primary role: Hedge against geopolitical uncertainty and currency devaluation.
   - Recommended allocation: typically 5% to 10% of total portfolio.

## Determining Your Asset Mix
A common rule of thumb for equity allocation is:
$$\text{Equity Allocation \%} \approx 100 - \text{Your Age} \quad (\text{or } 110 - \text{Age for higher risk tolerance})$$
- Example: At age 30, an investor might target approximately 70% in equities and 30% in debt/gold.
- At age 55, the mix shifts toward 45% equities and 55% fixed income to safeguard accumulated capital near retirement.

## The Importance of Annual Rebalancing
Market movements naturally distort target allocations over time:
- In a strong bull market, equities may surge from your target of 70% to 85% of your total net worth, exposing you to excessive downside risk.
- **Rebalancing**: Once a year (or when asset classes drift by more than $\pm 5\%$), sell a portion of the outperforming asset and buy the lagging asset to restore target weights.
- This enforces the golden investment discipline: systematically selling high and buying low without emotional speculation.
