# Sinking Funds for Predictable Periodic Expenses

**Author**: MoneyLens team  
**Last Updated**: 2026-10-15  
**Category**: Budgeting & Cash Flow  

## What is a Sinking Fund?
A sinking fund is a strategic savings technique where an individual sets aside a calculated, small sum of money every month into a dedicated sub-account to cover a known, predictable, non-monthly expense in the future.

Unlike an **Emergency Fund** (which is strictly preserved for unforeseen, unplanned emergencies like medical hospitalisation or sudden unemployment), a **Sinking Fund** is designated for planned, guaranteed future expenses that do not recur on a monthly billing cycle.

## Common Use Cases for Sinking Funds
- **Annual Insurance Premiums**: Car, two-wheeler, or health insurance premiums due once a year (e.g., ₹24,000 annual premium = ₹2,000/month sinking fund).
- **Festival Celebrations & Gifting**: Diwali, Eid, Christmas expenses, or family weddings.
- **Vehicle Maintenance**: Periodic scheduled car/bike servicing, tire replacements, or annual insurance renewal.
- **Home Maintenance & Property Taxes**: Quarterly or annual society maintenance charges and municipal property tax.
- **Annual Vacations & Travel**: Dedicated holiday savings accumulated over 12 months rather than swiped on a high-interest credit card.

## How to Set Up a Sinking Fund
1. **Identify the Total Target**: Calculate the expected total expense.
2. **Determine the Horizon**: Count the number of months remaining until the expense date.
3. **Calculate Monthly Contribution**:
   $$\text{Monthly Sinking Contribution} = \frac{\text{Expected Total Amount}}{\text{Months Until Expense}}$$
4. **Park in a High-Liquidity Instrument**: Keep sinking funds in a separate zero-fee savings account, recurring deposit (RD), or liquid mutual fund to prevent mingling with everyday operating checking accounts.

## Why Sinking Funds Protect Financial Health
When predictable annual expenses arrive, individuals without sinking funds often face sudden cash crunches, forcing them to raid their emergency savings or incur high-cost credit card debt. Sinking funds transform large, intimidating annual bills into manageable, routine monthly line items.
