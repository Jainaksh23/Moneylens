# The 50/30/20 Budgeting Framework

**Author**: MoneyLens team  
**Last Updated**: 2026-10-15  
**Category**: Budgeting & Cash-Flow Management  

## What is the 50/30/20 Rule?
The 50/30/20 rule is an intuitive, proportional budgeting guideline designed to help individuals balance immediate living requirements, lifestyle desires, and long-term financial security. It divides post-tax monthly net income into three distinct buckets:

1. **50% for Needs (Essential Living Expenses)**:
   - Expenses required for basic survival and maintaining livelihood.
   - Includes rent, home maintenance, utility bills (electricity, water, broadband), groceries, health insurance premiums, commuting costs, and minimum mandatory debt payments (e.g., student loan or home loan EMIs).
   - If needs exceed 50%, consider downsizing fixed commitments or actively auditing recurring fixed bills.

2. **30% for Wants (Discretionary Spending)**:
   - Non-essential lifestyle choices that improve quality of life.
   - Includes dining out, food delivery orders, entertainment subscriptions (OTT, music streaming), vacations, shopping for hobbies, gadgets, and leisure activities.
   - This category provides the highest degree of flexibility during financial crunches.

3. **20% for Savings & Debt Acceleration**:
   - Building future security and eliminating non-mortgage liabilities.
   - Includes building emergency reserves, systematic investment plans (SIPs) in mutual funds, recurring deposits, retirement contributions (NPS, PPF), and making extra principal prepayments against high-interest loans.

## Adaptation for Indian Urban Realities
In metropolitan Indian cities (e.g., Mumbai, Bengaluru, Delhi NCR), high rental costs often push essential needs to 55-60%. In such scenarios, a temporary **60/20/20** or **50/20/30** distribution can be adopted to preserve savings discipline while compressing discretionary leisure expenses.

## Core Principle
Budgeting is not about deprivation; it is conscious allocation. When you automate the 20% savings first on payday (the "Pay Yourself First" rule), you can spend the remaining discretionary funds without guilt.
