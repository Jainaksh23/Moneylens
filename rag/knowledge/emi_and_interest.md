# Understanding Equated Monthly Installments (EMI) and Interest

**Author**: MoneyLens team  
**Last Updated**: 2026-10-15  
**Category**: Debt & Loans  

## How an EMI Works
An Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) is a fixed payment amount made by a borrower to a lender on a specified calendar date each month. Each EMI payment is mathematically partitioned into two components:
1. **Principal Repayment**: Reduces the outstanding loan balance.
2. **Interest Charge**: Cost paid to the lender for borrowing capital, calculated on the outstanding balance.

## The Reducing Balance Method
In almost all modern retail loans (home loans, auto loans, personal loans) in India, interest is charged on a *monthly reducing balance*.
- In the initial years of a loan, the outstanding principal is at its maximum, meaning the interest chunk constitutes the majority of the monthly EMI.
- As regular EMIs gradually pay down the principal, the interest share declines and the principal repayment share surges.
- **Example**: In a 20-year home loan, up to 70-80% of your EMI in the first 3 years pays interest rather than loan principal.

## Why Flat Rate Interest Loans are Misleading
Some consumer durable loans or auto dealers advertise "Flat Interest Rates" (e.g., 7% flat rate).
- Under a flat rate, interest is calculated on the *initial* total principal for the entire loan tenure, ignoring the fact that you repay principal every month.
- An advertised 7% flat rate is approximately equivalent to a **13% to 14% effective reducing interest rate**. Always demand the *Effective Annualized Reducing Rate* (or APR) before signing.

## Prepayment Strategy to Slash Total Interest
Because interest is front-loaded, making voluntary principal prepayments in the early stages of a loan yields exponential savings:
- Making just **one extra EMI payment per year** on a 20-year home loan can reduce the overall loan duration by over 3 to 4 years and save lakhs of rupees in interest.
- Whenever you receive annual bonuses or surplus windfalls, channeling a portion toward principal reduction delivers a risk-free return equivalent to the loan's interest rate.
