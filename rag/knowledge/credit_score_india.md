# Credit Score Fundamentals in India

**Author**: MoneyLens team  
**Last Updated**: 2026-10-15  
**Category**: Credit & Borrowing  

## What is a Credit Score?
In India, a credit score is a 3-digit numerical summary (ranging from 300 to 900) representing an individual's creditworthiness and historical repayment reliability. Scores are computed by four RBI-licensed credit information bureaus:
1. **TransUnion CIBIL** (most widely used by Indian banks)
2. **Experian India**
3. **Equifax India**
4. **CRIF High Mark**

A score of **750 and above** is universally regarded as prime/excellent, qualifying borrowers for lower home loan interest rates, accelerated approvals, and higher credit limits.

## Key Factors That Determine Your Score
1. **Repayment History (~35% Weight)**:
   - Paying credit card bills and loan EMIs on or before the due date. Even a single 30-day default (DPD - Days Past Due) significantly damages your score for months.
2. **Credit Utilization Ratio - CUR (~30% Weight)**:
   - The proportion of total revolving credit limit utilized.
   - Best Practice: Maintain total credit card spends **under 30% of your total credit limit** across all active cards. Utilizing 80-90% signals credit hunger and depresses your score.
3. **Credit Age & History Length (~15% Weight)**:
   - Older credit accounts demonstrate proven reliability. Avoid closing your oldest credit card even if rarely used; keep it active with an occasional small charge.
4. **Credit Mix (~10% Weight)**:
   - A healthy balance between secured debt (home loan, car loan) and unsecured debt (credit cards, personal loans).
5. **Hard Inquiries (~10% Weight)**:
   - Applying for multiple loans or credit cards simultaneously triggers multiple "hard inquiries" by lenders, temporarily trimming score points.

## How to Remedy a Low Score
- Settle outstanding overdue balances rather than taking settlements with "written off" tags.
- Use a secured credit card backed by a fixed deposit (FD) if no unsecured card is approved.
- Check bureau reports quarterly for clerical errors or fraudulent identity theft accounts and raise formal disputes via bureau grievance portals.
